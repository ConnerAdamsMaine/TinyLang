# TIDE Moderation and Investigations

Private TIDE investigation reports are available at `/investigations.html`. Users with a session established by a linked GitHub, Discord, or X identity may submit reports and see only reports they opened. Designated investigators, granted explicitly through `tide_investigators`, can review the private queue, add evidence references, and record human decisions. General admin, maintainer, and moderator roles do not grant investigation access.

Reports receive permanent `TIDE-I-XXXXXX` identifiers. Case events, evidence references, investigator reviews, conflict declarations, and conflict-adjudication votes are append-only. An investigator may declare a personal relationship, financial interest, prior involvement, or another conflict, with a rationale. The declaration is visible to investigators and permanently recuses that investigator from adding evidence, uploading evidence, reviewing the case, or deciding its appeal. Independent investigators who did not open or target the case, declare a conflict, or review the case/appeal may assess whether a declaration is substantiated. Two matching votes finalize this assessment; split votes stay open for additional eligible reviewers. This adjudication records an assessment only: it never removes the declaration, lifts recusal, or changes an existing case decision. The API serializes declarations, adjudications, and case actions on the investigation row so recusal and eligibility checks cannot race past a write. A reviewer also cannot review a case they opened or a case targeting their own TIDE identity.

Automated detectors run transactionally when requests, bug reports, or engineering proposals are created. The near-duplicate rule looks back 24 hours within the same category and record type and signals only when both title and original-content trigram similarity meet the 0.82 threshold, at least four records match, and at least three TIDE accounts authored them. The rapid-submission-burst rule signals an account that creates at least eight request-type records in ten minutes. The possible-ban-evasion rule fires only when an account created within the last seven days submits content whose title and body both meet a strict 0.90 trigram-similarity threshold against a prior record authored by an account under an active TIDE restriction; it is deliberately content-only and never compares IP addresses, emails, usernames, OAuth subjects, or other account relationships. All three rules create private investigations with machine-generated evidence and metrics; an existing open case for the same user/rule suppresses further duplicate cases. The burst rule records no similarity score. Signals are leads for investigator review, not findings; no detector can suspend accounts or authorize punitive action. Broader detection requirements for governance manipulation, coordinated abuse, and large-scale spam are not implemented; ban evasion detection covers only this narrow content-reuse pattern and cannot see VPNs, new providers, or rewritten content.

A temporary suspension requires one recorded human investigator review. A permanent ban requires two distinct designated investigators to independently record the same action; the database enforces one review per investigator per case and the case transaction serializes review processing. Administrative accounts are excluded from this workflow and continue to use the existing superadmin review process. That legacy process only starts a restriction for an unrestricted account; confirmation requires an existing pending suspension and a different superadmin from the initial suspender. Legacy suspension and reinstatement routes cannot replace or clear a restriction linked to a TIDE enforcement action, preserving its investigation and appeal history. Removing an investigator grant revokes future investigator access without deleting historical review attribution.

When authorized enforcement is applied, active sessions are revoked and the user restriction, reviewer, rationale, and action are recorded transactionally with the investigation events. The review rationale records the policy reference and decision basis. Designated investigators who are not the report opener or target can attach up to ten private evidence files per case. Files are limited to 2 MiB each and PDF, PNG, JPEG, or plain text; their content signatures are checked and SHA-256 digests are recorded. Files are available only to authorized investigators, and downloads are sent as attachments with `nosniff` and `no-store` headers. Evidence is immutable: files cannot be manually deleted, and bytes are purged 90 days after a case is closed or appealed while append-only metadata, digests, and event history remain. Avoid credentials and unrelated personal data in reports and evidence.

Suspended users can request a single-use, 30-minute appeal link sent only to the verified email on the account. An appeal is bound to the exact active TIDE enforcement action, and only one appeal can be submitted for that action. Designated investigators can review the appeal queue; a reviewer who participated in or opened the original case, or who is the affected user, is excluded. Two distinct eligible investigators must record the same outcome before it takes effect. A split vote leaves the appeal open for additional eligible reviews; no restriction is changed until two votes match. Review rationales and vote events are append-only. Reversal clears the TIDE restriction and its active-action pointer transactionally; administrative restrictions superseding a TIDE action cannot be cleared through the appeal. A decided outcome is added to the account inbox and emailed to the affected account when email delivery is configured.

Limitations: conflict adjudication is an append-only assessment and does not provide an automatic remedy when a declaration is made after an earlier decision; the original decision remains in immutable history and affected users retain the appeal path. Investigators self-declare, and this workflow cannot compel a declaration. Email provider failures are logged but do not undo recorded decisions. Disposable-PostgreSQL integration tests cover investigator visibility, evidence authorization and retention, conflict recusal and independent matching-vote adjudication, near-duplicate, rapid-submission-burst, and content-only ban-evasion detection without restrictions (including the established-account negative control and open-case suppression), investigator revocation locking, permanent-ban review, split appeal votes remaining pending, and two matching independent appeal reviews. Appeal-email delivery remains untested; ticket and investigation attachments both have database-backed tests of their 90-day purge rules. The existing legacy moderator/admin API remains separate and is not a replacement for TIDE's investigation record.
